Why Good Decisions Beat Perfect Ones

Perfectionism feels safe, but it delays progress. A good decision made on time beats a perfect one that never ships.

Decision Compass Editorial November 18, 2025 5 min read

We treat decisions like exams with one correct answer. In real life, most choices are reversible, information is incomplete, and momentum matters as much as accuracy.

Perfection hides fear of regret

Waiting for certainty often means avoiding commitment. Name the worst realistic outcome if you choose wrong—usually it is manageable. That clarity frees you to act.

Good enough has a deadline

Set a decision date before you research. When it arrives, pick the best option on your shortlist. Extra days rarely change the winner; they drain energy you need for execution.

Reversible vs irreversible choices

Match effort to stakes. Lunch spots deserve minutes; leases and career pivots deserve structured comparison. Do not apply PhD-level analysis to reversible daily calls.

Progress teaches faster than research

After you decide, track results for two weeks. You learn more from one experiment than from ten more comparison articles. Good decisions plus feedback loops beat theoretical perfection.
